Still open

Narita both times — and which terminal?

Narita for both, so the cross-city transfer risk is gone — and sleeping at the airport is not merely convenient, it is what makes the morning possible: the first trains from central Tokyo do not reach Narita until about 06:30, so any Fukuoka departure before 08:30 is effectively unreachable from the city. Jetstar flies the route from 06:20 and Peach from 07:30. The one detail left is terminals: Jetstar goes from Terminal 3, and the pod hotels are in Terminal 2. They are connected by a walkway and a free shuttle, about fifteen minutes — worth knowing before you set an alarm.

The car booking is being redone

Kagoshima Airport at 13:00 is the agreed drop-off, and the 20th is built around it — Sekinoo, Kirishima Jingu and Kareigawa all fit before the counter closes on you. What is left is having that in writing on the new booking, because an earlier hour would cost the whole morning and leave seven hours in the terminal.

Kyoto and Tokyo are all that is left

Eight of the eighteen nights are booked. The remaining ten — four in Kyoto from the 24th and six in Tokyo from the 28th — have dates and no beds. Kyoto is the one to move on, because late November is peak maple season there and it is the leg where rooms genuinely run out.

The 2026 Kumamoto earthquake

A magnitude 7.1 quake hit Kumamoto Prefecture on 28 July 2026, reaching shindo 7 — the top of the Japanese scale. Kurokawa, Nabegataki, Aso, Kikuchi and Kumamoto itself are all inside that prefecture, so most of the Kyushu leg sits in the affected area. What is known: Kumamoto Castle reopened in mid-August with new stone-wall damage, and Kikuchi Gorge was closed and has since had its entry ban lifted. Every route on this page plots normally, which suggests the main roads are open. But mountain sites here have been opening and closing, so check each one close to the date via the Japan Tourism Agency and JNTO status pages — and keep the free-cancellation bookings free-cancellation.

Deadlines before you fly

Two bookings that sell out, and one free-cancellation window that closes. All three cut off before you land.

Takachiho Gorge rental boat — 19 Nov opens 5 Nov · shuts 17 Nov 09:00

The window opens exactly two weeks ahead and closes 9:00 AM two days before your slot. Online only — no phone, no time-slot requests, no waitlist. Same-day tickets are sold at the gorge reception in the morning only. Operating hours are decided each morning around 8:00 on water level and weather.

Nabegataki Falls — 14:00 or 15:00 on the 17th refundable until 14 Nov

Advance online booking only, in timed slots. ¥300 an adult, 9:00–17:00 with last entry 16:30, capped at 40 minutes to an hour with no re-entry. Book it for arrival day rather than the 18th — that swap is what makes the crater fit.

Aoshima, Rakuten STAY HOUSE 102 — free cancellation ends 13 Nov 23:59

Booked by Ricardo, €174 for the night of the 19th. Drop it free up to this point and it costs nothing; after it you are committed. The last date on which night 3 is still a decision.

20Fri

The inland run to the airport

Car back 13:00 · flight 17:00 · Osaka 20–24

  • Sekinoo Falls, MiyakonojoPotholes among the largest in the world, a National Natural Monument since 1928, with suspension bridges over the gorge. Free entry and parking. Budget 30 minutes for the falls alone, 50 with the potholes, 60 for the full loop. Food outlets can be shut midweek, and the 20th is a Friday.
  • Kirishima JinguA shrine in deep cedar forest on the volcano's flank, twenty minutes from the airport. Around 45 minutes to an hour.
  • Kareigawa StationOpened 1903, the oldest wooden station building in Kyushu and a National Registered Cultural Property since 2006 — original waiting room and ticket counter still in place. Ten minutes from the airport, so it is the natural last stop. Fifteen minutes is enough.
1 h 34 m · 127 km Aoshima → Kagoshima Airport, nothing on the way
1 h 58 m all three stops — twenty-four minutes more driving

All three stops fit, with a quarter of an hour more in bed. Leave Aoshima 08:15 · Sekinoo Falls 09:10 to 10:00 · Kirishima Jingu 10:50 to 11:40 · Kareigawa Station 12:15 to 12:30 · airport 12:40, car back by 13:00. Aoshima is 55 minutes from Sekinoo against Miyazaki's 70, so the same schedule starts fifteen minutes later.

Then four hours at the airport, which is better than it sounds. Kagoshima Airport has Oyattosa, a free footbath of real thermal water on the ground floor of the domestic terminal by Exit 3, open 09:00 to 19:30. The third-floor observation deck looks down the runway to Sakurajima smoking in the distance, and SORA STAGE has aircraft exhibits and a flight simulator. A reasonable way to finish Kyushu.

Day 17: the long way

Everything competes for one deadline — Nabegataki's last entry at 16:30. With the car at 10:00 that is six and a half hours to spend, and each option eats into it. Measured:

The suggestion: skip Kumamoto, take Dazaifu and Kurume. Car at 10:00, Dazaifu 10:30 to 11:45, Kurume for lunch 12:20 to 13:15, Nabegataki 14:45 to 15:45, DeepSpot by about 16:05 — in good time for the village baths. Two stops that are properly memorable, and still an easy day after a night in an airport.

Kumamoto costs 30 minutes more than both of those together and what you get is a castle mid-reconstruction: it reopened in August 2026 after the earthquake with new stone-wall damage, and the rebuild runs to 2052. Worth it if a great castle is the point; not worth it as a stop on the way somewhere.

And if you want Kikuchi Gorge — 11 minutes of driving but an hour of walking — the only honest way is to move Nabegataki to the 18th and drop the Nakadake crater. That is a real trade, not an addition.

What is actually worth stopping for

  • Dazaifu Tenmangu — 30 min from the airportOne of the country's principal shrines, in wooded grounds. The draw for a first day is the approach: around thirty shops grilling umegae mochi to order — red bean paste inside, pressed on a hot iron stamped with a plum blossom, crisp outside and chewy within. Eat it walking, straight off the griddle.
  • Kurume — lunch, on the expresswayTonkotsu ramen was invented here, at a stall called Nankin Senryo, in 1937. The milky white broth everyone now knows came ten years later and by accident, when a cook at a shop called Sankyu left the pot on too long and boiled the bones to emulsion. Eating it in the town that invented it is a better story than most museums.
  • Kumamoto Castle — the +1 h 18 m optionOne of Japan's three great castles, seen mid-repair. Sakuranobaba Josaien at its foot is the easy lunch; Suizenji Jojuen, an Edo strolling garden built as a miniature Tokaido complete with a small Mount Fuji, is 30 minutes away.
  • Kikuchi Gorge — only with the tradeFour kilometres of clear water and moss under broadleaf forest, one of Kumamoto's best autumn walks, and mid-November is the week. Closed after the quake, entry ban since lifted.

Leg 2 · Osaka

20–24 November · four nights. You land at Kansai at 18:00 on the 20th and leave for Nara and Kyoto on the 24th. One day is fixed, Universal Studios on Monday the 23rd. The other two are open.

All three full days fall inside a national long weekend. Monday 23 November is Labour Thanksgiving Day, so Saturday to Monday is a three-day holiday for the whole country. That is the single fact that shapes this leg: Universal will be at peak pricing and peak crowds whichever day you pick, and the city will be busy with domestic visitors rather than only tourists.

The city on foot

Open · a suggestion, not a plan

  • Kuromon Ichiba — morningA covered market of grilled scallops, uni and tuna eaten standing up. Go early: many stalls wind down through the afternoon rather than staying open to evening.
  • Osaka Castle and NishinomaruThe keep is a reconstruction and the queue for the lift is the cost of it. The garden below is the better hour, and late November is the end of the colour.
  • Shinsekai and TsutenkakuThe deliberately shabby old quarter, built to look like somewhere else entirely. Kushikatsu is the thing to eat, and the rule is you do not double-dip the sauce.
  • Abeno Harukas — sunsetThe tallest building in Japan when it opened, and the cheapest way to understand how flat and how endless Osaka is. Sunset is about 16:50 in late November.

The room split was the whole argument. Two doubles was ruled out, and Booking quotes two doubles by default, so every headline price on its search pages was the wrong configuration. Priced as three rooms, the Minami floor was €730 for two rooms and €877 for three. The third room costs so little because a room for one prices below a room for two.

A house was the cheaper answer and was not taken. Three bedrooms on Airbnb ran €259 to €403 for the same four nights, under half the hotel. It buys the same three doors but one and a half bathrooms between four adults, a shared kitchen, and nobody to call when something breaks. Privacy was the point of this leg, so the hotel won.

Hold the cancellation date. Free until 17 November. Worth knowing that the same three rooms now quote €965, up from the €877 booked, so the window is protecting real money rather than a formality.
